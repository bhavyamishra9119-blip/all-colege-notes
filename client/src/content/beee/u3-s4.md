- **Main Types of Clampers:** 

### 1. Positive Clamper (Upward Level Shift): Shunt diode points UP (anode grounded, cathode

at top rail).
- **During negative peak (−Vm):** diode turns ON → C charges to VC = Vm (right plate +).
- **For all cycles afterward:** vout = vin + Vm → shifts input [−Vm, +Vm] upward to [0 V, +2Vm].

### 2. Negative Clamper (Downward Level Shift): Shunt diode points DOWN (anode at top rail,

cathode grounded).
- **During positive peak (+Vm):** diode turns ON → C charges to VC = Vm (left plate +).
- **For all cycles afterward:** vout = vin − Vm → shifts input [−Vm, +Vm] downward to
[−2Vm, 0 V].

- **### 3. Biased Clamper:** Adding a DC source VR in series with the diode clamps the waveform

peak cleanly to +VR or −VR instead of 0 V.
- **Real-World Applications:** Restoring DC brightness reference in television video receivers,
voltage doubler/multiplier power supplies, and radar pulse timing.

### 8. Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)


- **### 8.1 Starting From Scratch:** What Is a Transistor?

- **Origin of the Name:** Transfer + Resistor — a device that transfers an input signal from a low-
resistance input side to a high-resistance output side, creating large voltage and power
amplification.
Why Called "Bipolar"? Electrical conduction inside the device involves both charge-carrier
polarities — negatively charged electrons (e−) and positively charged holes (h+).
- **Three-Layer, Two-Junction Device:** Built from three alternately doped regions on one crystal,
- **creating two p–n junctions:** 
Emitter–Base Junction (JE)
Collector–Base Junction (JC)
- **Current-Controlled Action:** A tiny control current at the base (IB in μA) regulates a large output
current at the collector (IC in mA).

### 8.2 The Three Terminals — Doping, Size & Physical Role


- **### 1. Emitter (E):** 

- **Doping Level:** Heavily doped (N++ or P++).
- **Physical Area:** Moderate.
- **Role:** Injects (emits) a dense supply of majority carriers into the base region.

- **### 2. Base (B):** 

- **Doping Level:** Lightly doped.
- **Physical Width:** Extremely thin (≈ 1 μm).
- **Role:** Passes over 95% of emitter-injected carriers straight across to the collector while keeping
recombination below 5%.

- **### 3. Collector (C):** 

- **Doping Level:** Moderately doped.
- **Physical Area:** Largest of all three regions.
- **Role:** Collects carriers arriving from the base and dissipates the heat produced at the reverse-
biased JC junction.
Why Emitter and Collector Cannot Be Swapped: Because the Emitter is heavily doped with
moderate area whereas the Collector is moderately doped with the largest area, a BJT is physically
asymmetrical. Reversing them yields extremely poor current gain (Inverse Active Mode).
- **UNIT–3:** Semiconductor Devices & Applications and Transistors


### 8.3 The Four Operating Regions (Modes) of a BJT


### 1. Active Region (JE Forward-Biased, JC Reverse-Biased): Collector current scales linearly

with base current (IC = βIB) → used for Linear Signal Amplification.

### 2. Saturation Region (JE Forward-Biased, JC Forward-Biased): Maximum current flows with

tiny drop VCE(sat) ≈ 0.2 V → acts as a Closed (ON) Electronic Switch.

### 3. Cut-off Region (JE Reverse-Biased, JC Reverse-Biased): Both junctions block conduction

(IC ≈ 0, VCE ≈ VCC) → acts as an Open (OFF) Electronic Switch.

### 4. Inverse Active Region (JE Reverse-Biased, JC Forward-Biased): Collector acts as emitter;

produces very weak gain → used only in specialized attenuators/digital TTL gates.

### 9. Construction, Symbol, and Working of PNP and NPN Transistors


### 9.1 Construction & Symbol Identification Rules

- **NPN Transistor:** 
- **Construction:** Ultra-thin, lightly doped P-type base sandwiched between a heavily doped N-
type emitter (N++) and a moderately doped, wide N-type collector (N).
- **Symbol Arrow Rule:** Arrow on the emitter lead points OUTWARD away from the base (easy
- **memory trick:** "Not Pointing iN").
- **Arrow Meaning:** Shows conventional emitter current (IE) flowing out of the emitter (opposite to
electron motion).
- **PNP Transistor:** 
- **Construction:** Ultra-thin, lightly doped N-type base sandwiched between a heavily doped P-
type emitter (P++) and a moderately doped, wide P-type collector (P).
- **Symbol Arrow Rule:** Arrow on the emitter lead points INWARD toward the base (easy memory
- **trick:** "Points iN Proudly").
- **Arrow Meaning:** Shows conventional emitter current (IE) flowing into the emitter (same
direction as hole motion).
- **UNIT–3:** Semiconductor Devices & Applications and Transistors


### 9.2 Step-by-Step Working of an NPN Transistor (Active Mode)


- **### 1. Step 1 — External Biasing:** 

- **Battery VEE forward-biases JE:** negative (−) to N-emitter, positive (+) to P-base.
- **Battery VCC reverse-biases JC:** positive (+) to N-collector, negative (−) to P-base.

### 2. Step 2 — Electron Injection from Emitter: Forward bias lowers the barrier at JE; the heavily

doped N-emitter injects a massive flood of free electrons into the P-base (forming Emitter Current
IE).

### 3. Step 3 — Tiny Base Recombination (< 5%): Because the P-base is extremely thin and lightly

doped, fewer than 5% of electrons recombine with holes there → creates a very small Base
Current (IB in μA).

### 4. Step 4 — Collection by Collector (> 95%): Over 95% of electrons cross the thin base and reach

the reverse-biased JC depletion zone, where the strong positive +VCC sweeps them rapidly into
the collector → creates a large Collector Current (IC in mA).

### 5. Step 5 — Kirchhoff Current Balance: Conventional IB and IC enter the transistor while IE leaves

→ IE = IB + IC.

### 9.3 Step-by-Step Working of a PNP Transistor (Active Mode)


- **### 1. Step 1 — External Biasing:** 

- **Battery VEE forward-biases JE:** positive (+) to P-emitter, negative (−) to N-base.
- **Battery VCC reverse-biases JC:** negative (−) to P-collector, positive (+) to N-base.

### 2. Step 2 — Hole Injection from Emitter: Forward bias drives a large stream of majority holes from

the heavily doped P-emitter into the thin N-base (constituting IE).

### 3. Step 3 — Tiny Base Recombination (< 5%): Fewer than 5% of holes recombine with electrons

in the lightly doped N-base → generates a small outward Base Current (IB).

### 4. Step 4 — Collection by Collector (> 95%): More than 95% of holes traverse the thin base and

are pulled into the collector by negative −VCC → produces a large outward Collector Current (IC).

### 5. Step 5 — Kirchhoff Current Balance: Conventional IE enters while IB and IC leave →

IE = IB + IC.

### 9.4 Why NPN Transistors Are Preferred Over PNP in Industry

- **Higher Mobility:** Free electrons in Silicon move nearly 3× faster (μn ≈ 1350 cm2/V·s) than holes
(μp ≈ 480 cm2/V·s).
- **Faster Speed & Bandwidth:** Because NPN transports electrons as primary carriers, it switches
faster and operates at higher frequencies.
Positive Ground-Referenced Supplies: NPN circuits naturally operate with standard positive
+VCC power rails used in almost all modern electronics.

### 10. Input and Output Characteristics of BJT in Different Configurations


### 10.1 Why Three Configurations Exist

- **The Two-Port Rule:** Any electronic circuit needs 2 input wires and 2 output wires (4 connections),
while a BJT has only 3 leads (E, B, C).
- **Solution:** One terminal is shared as the common ground/reference between input and output,
- **yielding 3 configurations:** 

### 1. Common Base (CB) — Base is common.


### 2. Common Emitter (CE) — Emitter is common (most widely used).


### 3. Common Collector (CC) — Collector is common (known as Emitter Follower).

Early Effect (Base-Width Modulation) — Key Concept Behind Curve Shifts:
When reverse voltage across the collector junction (VCB or VCE) increases, the collector
depletion layer widens deeper into the thin base.
This shrinks the effective base width → fewer carriers recombine in the base → IC rises slightly
and IB drops slightly.

### 10.2 Common Base (CB) Configuration

- **Terminals:** Input = Emitter & Base (VEB, IE)  |  Output = Collector & Base (VCB, IC).
Input Characteristics (IE vs. VEB at constant VCB):
- **Looks like a forward-biased diode:** IE ≈ 0 until VEB ≈ 0.7 V (Si), then increases steeply.
Raising VCB narrows the base (Early effect), letting more emitter current flow at the same VEB
→ curve shifts slightly left.
- **Input Resistance:** ri = ΔVEB / ΔIE → very low (20 – 50 Ω).
Output Characteristics (IC vs. VCB at constant IE):
Active Region (VCB > 0): Nearly flat horizontal lines; IC ≈ αIE ≈ IE independent of VCB.
Saturation Region (VCB < 0): JC becomes forward-biased; IC plunges sharply to zero.
Cut-off Region (below IE = 0): Only tiny leakage current ICBO flows.
- **Output Resistance:** ro = ΔVCB / ΔIC → very high (100 kΩ – 1 MΩ).

### 10.3 Common Emitter (CE) Configuration (Most Popular)

- **Terminals:** Input = Base & Emitter (VBE, IB)  |  Output = Collector & Emitter (VCE, IC).
