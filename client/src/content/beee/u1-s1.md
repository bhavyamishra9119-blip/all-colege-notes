### 1. Voltage and Current Sources


#### 1.1 Starting From Scratch: Active vs. Passive Elements & Source Classification

**Direct Current (DC):** Electric current whose magnitude and direction remain constant with time
(frequency f = 0 Hz); supplied by batteries, solar cells, and DC power supplies.
**Active Elements (Sources):** Devices capable of delivering electrical energy continuously to a
circuit (Voltage & Current Sources).
**Independent Sources:** Output voltage or current is fixed and does not depend on any other
voltage or current elsewhere in the circuit (drawn with a circle symbol).
**Dependent (Controlled) Sources:** Output depends linearly on a voltage or current in another
branch (drawn with a diamond symbol):
VCVS (Voltage-Controlled Voltage Source), CCVS (Current-Controlled Voltage Source), VCCS
(Voltage-Controlled Current Source), and CCCS (Current-Controlled Current Source).

#### 1.2 Ideal vs. Practical Voltage Source


**Ideal Voltage Source:**

Maintains a strictly constant terminal voltage (VL = Vs) regardless of how much load current
(IL) is drawn.
**Internal Series Resistance:** Rs = 0 Ω (impossible physically, used as an ideal model).

**Practical (Real) Voltage Source:**

Modeled as an ideal voltage source Vs in series with a small internal resistance Rs.
**Terminal Voltage Equation:** VL = Vs − IL Rs — terminal voltage droops slightly as load current
IL increases (e.g., car battery dimming headlights when cranking starter).

#### 1.3 Ideal vs. Practical Current Source


**Ideal Current Source:**

Supplies a strictly constant load current (IL = Is) regardless of the voltage across its terminals
or load resistance value.
**Internal Parallel Resistance:** Rp = ∞ Ω (infinite shunt resistance).

**Practical (Real) Current Source:**

Modeled as an ideal current source Is in parallel with a high internal shunt resistance Rp.

#### 2.1 Fundamental Circuit Topology Terms

**Node:** Any connection point where two or more circuit elements meet (a Junction connects 3 or
more branches).
**Branch:** A single circuit element or path connecting two adjacent nodes.
**Loop:** Any closed conducting path in a circuit; a Mesh is an elementary loop containing no other
loops inside it.
