## PART I: DC CIRCUITS AND NETWORK THEOREMS

### 1. Voltage and Current Sources

#### 1.1 Starting From Scratch: Active vs. Passive Elements & Source Classification

* **Direct Current (DC):** Electric current whose magnitude and direction remain strictly constant with time (frequency $f = 0\text{ Hz}$); supplied by chemical batteries, solar photovoltaic cells, and regulated DC power supplies.
* **Active Elements (Sources):** Devices capable of delivering electrical energy continuously to a circuit (Voltage & Current Sources).
* **Independent Sources:** Output voltage or current is fixed and does not depend on any other voltage or current elsewhere in the circuit (drawn with a circle symbol).
* **Dependent (Controlled) Sources:** Output depends linearly on a voltage or current in another branch of the network (drawn with a diamond symbol):
  * **VCVS:** Voltage-Controlled Voltage Source
  * **CCVS:** Current-Controlled Voltage Source
  * **VCCS:** Voltage-Controlled Current Source
  * **CCCS:** Current-Controlled Current Source

#### 1.2 Ideal vs. Practical Voltage Source

* **Ideal Voltage Source:** Maintains a strictly constant terminal voltage ($V_L = V_s$) regardless of how much load current ($I_L$) is drawn.
  * **Internal Series Resistance:** $R_s = 0\,\Omega$ (physically impossible, used as an ideal reference model).
* **Practical (Real) Voltage Source:** Modeled as an ideal voltage source $V_s$ in series with a small internal resistance $R_s$.
  * **Terminal Voltage Equation:**
  $$V_L = V_s - I_L R_s$$
  * *Physical Intuition:* Terminal voltage droops slightly as load current $I_L$ increases (e.g., a car battery dimming headlights when the starter motor cranks).

#### 1.3 Ideal vs. Practical Current Source

* **Ideal Current Source:** Supplies a strictly constant load current ($I_L = I_s$) regardless of the voltage across its terminals or load resistance value.
  * **Internal Parallel Resistance:** $R_p = \infty\,\Omega$ (infinite internal shunt resistance).
* **Practical (Real) Current Source:** Modeled as an ideal current source $I_s$ in parallel with a high internal shunt resistance $R_p$.
  * **Load Current Equation:**
  $$I_L = I_s - \frac{V_L}{R_p}$$
  * *Physical Intuition:* Delivered load current drops slightly as terminal voltage $V_L$ rises.

#### 1.4 Source Transformation (Practical Voltage ↔ Practical Current Source)

A practical voltage source ($V_s$ in series with $R$) can be replaced at terminals A–B by an equivalent practical current source ($I_s$ in parallel with the exact same resistance $R$) and vice versa:

* **Voltage → Current Source:** $I_s = \frac{V_s}{R}$ with $R$ placed in parallel.
* **Current → Voltage Source:** $V_s = I_s \times R$ with $R$ placed in series.
* **Quick Worked Example:** A $12\text{ V}$ source in series with $4\,\Omega$ transforms directly into an equivalent $12 / 4 = 3\text{ A}$ current source in parallel with $4\,\Omega$!

| Parameter | Ideal Voltage Source | Practical Voltage Source | Ideal Current Source | Practical Current Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Internal Resistance** | Zero ($0\,\Omega$) | Very Low (in Series $R_s$) | Infinite ($\infty\,\Omega$) | Very High (in Shunt $R_p$) |
| **Terminal Behavior** | $V_L = V_s$ (Constant) | $V_L = V_s - I_L R_s$ | $I_L = I_s$ (Constant) | $I_L = I_s - \frac{V_L}{R_p}$ |
| **Deactivation Rule** | Short Circuit ($0\text{ V}$) | Replace $V_s$ by Short (Keep $R_s$) | Open Circuit ($0\text{ A}$) | Replace $I_s$ by Open (Keep $R_p$) |

| Parameter / Feature | Ideal Voltage Source | Practical Voltage Source | Ideal Current Source | Practical Current Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Internal Resistance** | $R_s = 0\,\Omega$ (Zero Series) | Small series resistance ($R_s > 0$) | $R_p = \infty\,\Omega$ (Infinite Shunt) | High parallel resistance ($R_p < \infty$) |
| **Terminal Voltage ($V_L$)** | Constant ($V_L = V_s$) $\forall\ I_L$ | $V_L = V_s - I_L R_s$ (Droops) | Depends entirely on Load $R_L$ | $V_L = I_L R_L$ |
| **Load Current ($I_L$)** | Determined solely by Load | $I_L = V_s / (R_s + R_L)$ | Strictly Constant ($I_L = I_s$) | $I_L = I_s - (V_L / R_p)$ |
| **V–I Characteristic** | Horizontal straight line | Slight downward slope | Vertical straight line | Slight negative slope |
| **Real-World Example** | Theoretical Benchmark | Chemical battery, Lab DC Supply | Norton equivalent model | Transistor in active region |
