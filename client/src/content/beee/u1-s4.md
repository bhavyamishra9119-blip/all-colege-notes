## PART II: SINGLE-PHASE AC CIRCUITS, RESONANCE, POWER & APPLICATIONS

### 6. Fundamentals of Alternating Current (AC)

#### 6.1 Starting From Scratch: Sinusoidal AC Generation & Equation

* **Sinusoidal Waveform Equation:**
$$v(t) = V_m \sin(\omega t) = V_m \sin(2\pi f t)$$
$$i(t) = I_m \sin(\omega t \pm \phi)$$
  * $V_m, I_m$: Peak (Maximum / Amplitude) value.
  * $\omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T}$: Angular frequency in radians per second (for Indian $50\text{ Hz}$ supply: $\omega = 314.16\text{ rad/s}$).
  * $f$: Linear frequency in Hertz ($50\text{ Hz}$ = 50 complete cycles per second).
  * $\phi$: Phase angle in radians or electrical degrees.

#### 6.2 Essential AC Waveform Terms & Quantitative Values

* **Average Value ($V_{\text{avg}}$):** Average over one positive half-cycle:
$$V_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\pi V_m \sin(\omega t) d(\omega t) = \frac{2 V_m}{\pi} \approx 0.637 V_m$$
* **Root Mean Square / RMS Value ($V_{\text{rms}}$):** Effective DC equivalent heating value:
$$V_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} V_m^2 \sin^2(\omega t) d(\omega t)} = \frac{V_m}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 V_m$$
* **Form Factor ($K_f$):** Ratio of RMS value to Average value:
$$K_f = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{V_{\text{avg}}} = \frac{0.707 V_m}{0.637 V_m} = \frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}} = 1.11$$
* **Peak / Crest Factor ($K_p$):** Ratio of Maximum value to RMS value:
$$K_p = \frac{V_m}{V_{\text{rms}}} = \frac{V_m}{V_m / \sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2} = 1.414$$
*(Crucial in sizing cable insulation against dielectric breakdown!)*

### 7. Analysis of Single-Phase AC Circuits (R, L, C & Combinations)

#### 7.1 Behavior of Pure R, Pure L, and Pure C Circuits

* **1. Pure Resistive Circuit (R Only):**
  * Voltage and Current are in phase: $\phi = 0^\circ$.
  * Opposition: Resistance $R$ (independent of frequency).
  * Average Power: $P = V I \cos 0^\circ = V I\text{ W}$.
* **2. Pure Inductive Circuit (L Only):**
  * Current **LAGS** voltage by $90^\circ$ (Memory mnemonic: **ELI** — EMF leads Current in L).
  * Opposition = Inductive Reactance: $X_L = \omega L = 2\pi f L\,\Omega$ (increases linearly with frequency).
  * Average Power: $P = V I \cos 90^\circ = 0\text{ W}$ (energy periodically stored and returned via magnetic field).
* **3. Pure Capacitive Circuit (C Only):**
  * Current **LEADS** voltage by $90^\circ$ (Memory mnemonic: **ICE** — Current leads EMF in C).
  * Opposition = Capacitive Reactance: $X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C} = \frac{1}{2\pi f C}\,\Omega$ (inversely proportional to frequency).
  * Average Power: $P = V I \cos(-90^\circ) = 0\text{ W}$ (energy stored and returned via electrostatic field).

#### 7.2 Series Combinations: R–L, R–C, and R–L–C Circuits

In a series R–L–C circuit:
* **Total Net Reactance:** $X = X_L - X_C$
* **Circuit Impedance Magnitude:**
$$Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$$
* **Phase Angle:** $\phi = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{X_L - X_C}{R}\right)$
* **Three Operating States:**
  1. $X_L > X_C$: Net Inductive circuit $\to$ Current lags voltage.
  2. $X_C > X_L$: Net Capacitive circuit $\to$ Current leads voltage.
  3. $X_L = X_C$: Series Resonance $\to$ Net reactance $X = 0$, $Z = R$, Unity Power Factor ($\,\phi = 0^\circ$).

<example title="Series R–L–C Numerical Solution" level="medium">
A coil ($R = 30\,\Omega, X_L = 60\,\Omega$) is connected in series with a capacitor ($X_C = 20\,\Omega$) across a $200\text{ V}, 50\text{ Hz}$ supply.
1. **Net Reactance:** $X = X_L - X_C = 60 - 20 = 40\,\Omega\text{ (Inductive)}$
2. **Impedance:** $Z = \sqrt{30^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{900 + 1600} = 50\,\Omega$
3. **Current:** $I = \frac{V}{Z} = \frac{200}{50} = 4\text{ A}$
4. **Power Factor:** $\cos\phi = \frac{R}{Z} = \frac{30}{50} = 0.6\text{ lagging}$
5. **Active Power:** $P = V I \cos\phi = 200 \times 4 \times 0.6 = 480\text{ W}$
</example>
