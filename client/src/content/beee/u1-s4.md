### 6. Fundamentals of Alternating Current (AC)


#### 6.1 Starting From Scratch: Sinusoidal AC Generation & Equation

What Is Alternating Current (AC)? An electric voltage or current whose magnitude varies
continuously and whose polarity/direction reverses periodically at fixed intervals.
**Why Sine Waves Are Universal:** Rotating a coil at uniform angular speed ω inside a uniform
magnetic field naturally induces a pure sinusoidal EMF (e = Em sin ωt); moreover, differentiating or
integrating a sine wave always yields another sine wave of the exact same frequency!
**Standard Instantaneous Equation:** v(t) = Vm sin(ωt ± φ) where Vm = Peak amplitude, ω = 2πf
(rad/s), and φ = Initial phase angle.

#### 6.2 Essential AC Waveform Terms & Quantitative Values

**Cycle & Time Period (T):** One complete set of positive + negative variations; time taken for one
cycle is T (seconds).
**Frequency (f):** Number of complete cycles per second: f = 1 / T (Hertz, Hz). Indian AC mains
**standard:** 50 Hz (T = 20 ms); USA standard: 60 Hz.
**Phase & Phase Difference (φ):** 
In-Phase (φ = 0°): Both waveforms reach zero and peak together.
**Leading (+φ):** Waveform reaches its peak earlier in time (sin(ωt + φ)).
**Lagging (−φ):** Waveform reaches its peak later in time (sin(ωt − φ)).

**RMS (Root Mean Square / Effective) Value:**

The equivalent steady DC current that produces the exact same heat in a given resistor over
one full cycle as the AC waveform.

### 7. Analysis of Single-Phase AC Circuits (R, L, C & Combinations)


#### 7.1 Behavior of Pure R, Pure L, and Pure C Circuits


### 1. Pure Resistive Circuit (R Only):


$$Opposition = Resistance R (independent of frequency).$$

**Phase Relation:** Current I is strictly in phase with Voltage V (φ = 0°, cos φ = 1).
**Average Power:** P = V I = I2 R (100% dissipated as heat).

### 2. Pure Inductive Circuit (L Only):

Opposition = Inductive Reactance: XL = ωL = 2πfL (Ω) — increases linearly with frequency (0
Ω for DC).
**Phase Relation:** Current LAGS voltage by 90° (Memory trick: ELI — EMF leads Current in L).
**Average Power:** P = V I cos 90° = 0 W (energy alternates between source and magnetic field).

### 3. Pure Capacitive Circuit (C Only):

Opposition = Capacitive Reactance: XC = 1

increases (∞ Ω blocks DC).
**Phase Relation:** Current LEADS voltage by 90° (Memory trick: ICE — Current leads EMF in
C).
**Average Power:** P = V I cos 90° = 0 W (energy alternates between source and electric field).

**Net Reactance & Impedance:** Because VL (+90°) and VC (−90°) oppose each other by 180°:

$$Z = R + j(XL − XC) → Magnitude |Z| = √(R2 + (XL − XC)2).$$

**Three Operating Regimes:**


### 1. XL > XC: Net Inductive circuit → Current lags voltage.


### 2. XC > XL: Net Capacitive circuit → Current leads voltage.


### 3. XL = XC: Series Resonance → Net reactance = 0, Z = R, Unity PF (φ = 0°).
