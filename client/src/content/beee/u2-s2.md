### 3. Ideal and Practical Transformer


- **### 3.1 Starting From Scratch:** Principle & Construction

- **Definition:** A Transformer is a static (zero moving parts) electromagnetic device that
transfers AC electrical power from one circuit (Primary) to another electrically isolated circuit
(Secondary) at the exact same frequency (f) while stepping voltage UP or DOWN.
Operating Principle — Faraday’s Law of Mutual Induction:

### 1. Alternating voltage V1 applied to Primary winding (N1 turns) drives an alternating magnetizing

current.

### 2. This current establishes a continuously changing magnetic flux Φ = Φm sin(ωt) inside the low-

reluctance silicon-steel core.

### 3. The alternating flux links the Secondary winding (N2 turns) and induces an alternating EMF e2 =

−N2 (dΦ/dt) without any direct electrical wire connection!
Why a Transformer NEVER Works on DC (Favorite Exam Question!):
DC produces a constant magnetic flux (dΦ / dt = 0) → zero induced EMF in the secondary!
- **Worse:** without opposing self-induced back EMF (E1), the very low winding resistance draws a
huge short-circuit DC current that burns out the primary winding!

- **Basic Construction Types:** 
- **Core-Type Transformer:** Cylindrical windings wrap around the two outer limbs of a rectangular
laminated core (easier insulation & repair; preferred for high-voltage power transformers).
- **Shell-Type Transformer:** Both windings are sandwiched on the central limb surrounded by two
outer iron return paths (better mechanical protection & lower flux leakage; preferred for low-
voltage high-current applications).
Universal Transformer EMF Equation & Turns Ratio (Must Memorize!):
- **RMS Induced EMF:** E1 = 4.44 f N1 Φm   and   E2 = 4.44 f N2 Φm  (where Φm = Bm × A).

### 3.2 Ideal Transformer vs. Practical Transformer

Ideal Transformer (Lossless Theoretical Baseline): Assumes zero winding resistances (R1 = R2
= 0), zero magnetic leakage flux (100% flux links both coils), infinite core permeability (μr = ∞), and
zero core losses → Efficiency = 100% (V1I1 = V2I2) and V1 = E1, V2 = E2.
- **Practical (Real) Transformer:** Accounts for four unavoidable physical imperfections:

### 1. Finite Winding Resistances (R1, R2): Copper coils produce resistive voltage drops (I R) and

I2R heat losses.

### 2. Magnetic Leakage Reactances (X1, X2): Small portion of flux leaks through air instead of

linking both coils, causing reactive drops (I X).

### 3. Finite Core Permeability & No-Load Current (I0): Even at no load (I2 = 0), primary draws a

small current I0 (2%–5% of full load) with magnetizing component Im (builds flux) and core-loss
component Iw (supplies iron heat).

### 4. Core Losses & Voltage Regulation: Hysteresis + eddy current losses keep real efficiency at

95%–99%, and terminal voltage V2 drops slightly as load increases.

### 4. Equivalent Circuit of a Transformer


### 4.1 Building the Exact & Approximate Equivalent Circuits

- **Purpose:** Replaces magnetic coupling with an ordinary electric circuit of resistors and reactances
