### 3. Superposition Theorem


### 3.1 Statement, Applicability & Key Limitation


> 🟢 **Statement: In any linear, bilateral electrical network containing two or more independent**

sources, the resultant current through (or voltage across) any branch equals the algebraic sum of
the individual currents (or voltages) produced by each independent source acting alone, while
all other independent sources are deactivated.
Source Deactivation Rules (Must Memorize!):
Independent Voltage Source → Replaced by a Short Circuit (0 V) (keep its internal resistance
if given).
Independent Current Source → Replaced by an Open Circuit (0 A) (keep its internal
resistance if given).
- **Note:** Dependent sources are NEVER deactivated.
Critical Exam Limitation (Power Rule): Superposition applies ONLY to linear quantities (Current
I and Voltage V). It CANNOT be used directly to add Power because power is non-linear (P = I2R

### 4. Thevenin’s Theorem and Norton’s Theorem


### 4.1 Thevenin’s Theorem (Series Voltage Equivalent)


> 🟢 **Statement: Any complex two-terminal linear bilateral DC network can be replaced across load**

terminals A–B by a single equivalent Voltage Source (Vth) in series with a single equivalent
Resistance (Rth).
4-Step Procedure to Solve Any Thevenin Problem:

### 1. Remove Load RL across terminals A–B (leave A–B open-circuited).


### 2. Find Vth (Open-Circuit Voltage): Calculate the voltage VAB appearing across open terminals

A–B.

### 3. Find Rth (Thevenin Resistance): Deactivate all independent sources (Voltage → Short,

Current → Open) and calculate total resistance looking back into terminals A–B.

### 4. Reconnect RL & Compute Load Current: IL =

### 6. Fundamentals of Alternating Current (AC)


- **### 6.1 Starting From Scratch:** Sinusoidal AC Generation & Equation

What Is Alternating Current (AC)? An electric voltage or current whose magnitude varies
continuously and whose polarity/direction reverses periodically at fixed intervals.
- **Why Sine Waves Are Universal:** Rotating a coil at uniform angular speed ω inside a uniform
magnetic field naturally induces a pure sinusoidal EMF (e = Em sin ωt); moreover, differentiating or
integrating a sine wave always yields another sine wave of the exact same frequency!
- **Standard Instantaneous Equation:** v(t) = Vm sin(ωt ± φ) where Vm = Peak amplitude, ω = 2πf
(rad/s), and φ = Initial phase angle.

### 6.2 Essential AC Waveform Terms & Quantitative Values

- **Cycle & Time Period (T):** One complete set of positive + negative variations; time taken for one
cycle is T (seconds).
- **Frequency (f):** Number of complete cycles per second: f = 1 / T (Hertz, Hz). Indian AC mains
- **standard:** 50 Hz (T = 20 ms); USA standard: 60 Hz.
- **Phase & Phase Difference (φ):** 
In-Phase (φ = 0°): Both waveforms reach zero and peak together.
- **Leading (+φ):** Waveform reaches its peak earlier in time (sin(ωt + φ)).
- **Lagging (−φ):** Waveform reaches its peak later in time (sin(ωt − φ)).
RMS (Root Mean Square / Effective) Value:
The equivalent steady DC current that produces the exact same heat in a given resistor over
one full cycle as the AC waveform.
