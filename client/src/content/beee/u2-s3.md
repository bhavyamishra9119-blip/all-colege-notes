### 5. Losses in Transformers (and Maximum Efficiency Condition)


#### 5.1 Why Transformers Have No Mechanical Losses

Because a transformer has zero rotating parts, it has zero friction and windage losses —
giving it the highest efficiency (95%–99%) of all electrical machines. Its energy losses consist
almost entirely of two main electrical categories:

#### 5.2 Detailed Breakdown of Transformer Losses


### 1. Core Losses / Iron Losses (Pi — Constant Losses)

Occur inside the magnetic iron core due to alternating flux Φ; because supply voltage V1 and
frequency f stay constant, Iron Losses remain CONSTANT from no-load to full-load!
**(a) Hysteresis Loss (Ph):** Heat wasted reversing magnetic domains 50 times per second:
**Formula (Steinmetz):** Ph = kh Bm
1.6 f V (Watts).
**How to Minimize:** Build the core using high-grade CRGO Silicon Steel (narrow hysteresis
loop).
**(b) Eddy Current Loss (Pe):** Alternating flux induces swirling circulating currents (eddy currents)
inside solid metal iron, generating I2R core heat:

> 💡 **Formula: Pe = ke Bm**

2 f2 t2 V (Watts, where t = lamination thickness).
**How to Minimize:** Stack thin insulated laminations (0.35 – 0.5 mm thick) coated with
varnish/oxide — breaks up eddy paths and raises core resistance!

### 6. Open Circuit (OC) and Short Circuit (SC) Tests


#### 6.1 Why OC and SC Tests Are Preferred Over Direct Full-Load Testing

Directly loading a large 500 kVA power transformer in a lab wastes enormous electricity and
requires massive load banks.
Performing OC + SC indirect tests draws less than 5% of rated power yet accurately yields: (1)
Iron loss & Full-load copper loss, (2) Equivalent circuit parameters (R0, Xm, R01, X01), (3)
Efficiency at any load, and (4) Voltage Regulation!

#### 6.2 Open Circuit (No-Load) Test

**Setup Rule (Memorize!):** Meters (Voltmeter, Ammeter, Low-Power-Factor Wattmeter) are
connected on the Low-Voltage (LV) side (safe & convenient rated voltage); the High-Voltage
(HV) side is left OPEN.
**Supply Applied:** Rated Voltage (V0 = Vrated) at Rated Frequency.
