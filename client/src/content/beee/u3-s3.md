### 5. Ideal Diode


- **### 5.1 Concept:** The Perfect One-Way Electronic Switch

- **Definition:** An idealized theoretical model that treats a p–n diode as a lossless one-way switch,
enabling fast first-step circuit analysis without solving exponential equations.
Forward Bias (VD ≥ 0) → Closed Switch (Short Circuit):
Cut-in voltage Vγ = 0 V (conducts immediately at any tiny positive voltage).
Forward resistance rf = 0 Ω → zero voltage drop across the conducting diode (VD = 0 V).
V–I curve runs straight vertically up the positive +IF axis.
Reverse Bias (VD < 0) → Open Switch (Open Circuit):
Reverse resistance rr = ∞ → zero reverse leakage current (I0 = 0 A).
Infinite breakdown voltage (never breaks down).
V–I curve lies flat along the negative −VR axis.

### 5.2 The Three Diode Circuit Approximations (Beginner Problem-Solving Tool)

1st Approximation (Ideal Diode Model): Ideal switch only (Vγ = 0 V, rf = 0 Ω). Best when source
voltage Vs >> 0.7 V.
2nd Approximation (Constant Voltage Drop Model): Ideal switch in series with a small battery
Vγ (0.7 V Si / 0.3 V Ge). Most widely used in university exams.
3rd Approximation (Piecewise Linear Model): Ideal switch + battery Vγ + bulk forward
resistance rf. Used for precision high-current design.

### 6. Half-Wave, Full-Wave, and Bridge Rectifiers


### 6.1 Fundamentals of Rectification (From Scratch)

What is a Rectifier? An electronic circuit using diodes to convert bidirectional Alternating
Current (AC) into unidirectional pulsating Direct Current (DC).
Why Electronic Gadgets Need Rectifiers: Wall outlets supply AC (e.g., 230 V, 50 Hz), whereas
digital chips, phones, and DC circuits require steady DC.
- **Role of the Step-Down Transformer:** 
Reduces high AC mains voltage to the desired low AC peak voltage Vm.
Provides galvanic (electrical) isolation between dangerous mains wiring and the user circuit.

- **Construction:** Center-tapped transformer (splits secondary into two equal halves, each

- **ON, D2 is OFF → current path:** A → D1 → RL → O.

- **is OFF → current path:** B → D2 → RL → O.

continuous pulsating DC with zero dead gaps.

### 6.4 Full-Wave Bridge Rectifier

- **Construction:** Ordinary two-wire transformer (no center tap required) + 4 diodes
(D1, D2, D3, D4) arranged in a closed diamond bridge + load resistor RL across the DC output
corners.
- **Step-by-Step Operation:** 

- **### 1. Positive Half-Cycle (0 → π):** Secondary top A is +, bottom B is − → diagonal pair D1 & D2

turn ON; D3 & D4 stay OFF → current path: A → D1 → RL (top to bottom) → D2 → B.

### 2. Negative Half-Cycle (π → 2π): Terminal B is +, A is − → diagonal pair D3 & D4 turn ON;

- **D1 & D2 stay OFF → current path:** B → D3 → RL (top to bottom) → D4 → A.

- **### 3. Key Outcome:** Unidirectional full-wave load current identical in shape to the center-tapped

FWR.
Key Mathematical Results & Practical Trade-offs:
Same Vdc = 0.636 Vm, Vrms = 0.707 Vm, γ = 0.482, ηmax = 81.2%, and fout = 2fin.
- **Major Advantages:** Eliminates costly center-tapped transformer, halves diode stress to
PIV = Vm, and achieves the highest TUF = 0.812 (most popular rectifier in modern power
supplies).
- **Minor Drawback:** Because 2 diodes conduct in series each half-cycle, practical peak output
drops by 2Vγ ≈ 1.4 V (for Silicon).

### 7. Clipper and Clamper Circuits


### 7.1 Clipper Circuits (Voltage Limiters / Slicers)

- **Definition:** A diode–resistor wave-shaping circuit that slices off (removes) an unwanted portion of
an AC waveform above or below a selected voltage level without distorting the remaining part of
the waveform.
- **Essential Components:** Diode(s) + Resistor (no capacitor needed) + optional DC battery (VR) to
shift the clipping threshold.
Classification & Step-by-Step Analysis (Assuming Ideal Diode):

- **### 1. Series Positive Clipper:** Diode is in series with the load, cathode facing input.

- **Positive half-cycle:** Diode is reverse-biased (OFF/Open) → vout = 0 V (positive half clipped
off).
- **Negative half-cycle:** Diode is forward-biased (ON/Short) → vout = vin (passes intact).

- **### 2. Series Negative Clipper:** Diode is reversed (anode facing input).

- **Positive half-cycle:** Diode is ON → vout = vin.
- **Negative half-cycle:** Diode is OFF → vout = 0 V (negative half clipped off).
- **UNIT–3:** Semiconductor Devices & Applications and Transistors


### 3. Shunt (Parallel) Positive Clipper: Series resistor R followed by shunt diode across output

(anode on top, cathode grounded).
- **Positive half-cycle:** Diode turns ON (short-circuits output) → vout = 0 V (excess voltage drops
across R).
- **Negative half-cycle:** Diode turns OFF (open) → vout = vin.

### 4. Shunt (Parallel) Negative Clipper: Reverse the shunt diode (cathode on top, anode

grounded) → passes positive half and clips negative half to 0 V.

- **### 5. Biased Clipper:** Placing a DC reference battery VR in series with the diode moves the

clipping threshold from 0 V to +VR or −VR (e.g., a biased shunt positive clipper clips everything
above +VR).

### 6. Combinational (Two-Level) Clipper: Uses two oppositely oriented biased shunt diodes in

parallel to clip both positive peaks (at +VR1) and negative peaks (at −VR2) → converts a sine
wave into an approximate square wave.
- **Real-World Applications:** Protecting sensitive IC inputs from over-voltage spikes, removing
amplitude noise in FM receivers, and generating square/trapezoidal waves.

### 7.2 Clamper Circuits (DC Restorers / Level Shifters)

- **Definition:** A diode–capacitor–resistor circuit that shifts an entire AC waveform vertically upward
or downward to a desired DC reference level without changing its wave shape or peak-to-peak
swing (2Vm).
- **Mandatory Components:** Series Capacitor (C) + Shunt Diode (D) + Load Resistor (RL).
Golden Design Condition (Time Constant Rule):
The RC discharging time constant must be much larger than half the input period:
τ = RLC ≥ 10 × (T
2).
Why? This guarantees that the capacitor charges almost instantly while the diode is ON, and
holds its stored DC voltage nearly constant while the diode is OFF.
4-Step Beginner Method to Solve Any Clamper Circuit:

- **### 1. Step 1:** Start analysis with the input half-cycle that forward-biases the diode first.


- **### 2. Step 2:** During that half-cycle, replace the ON diode with a short circuit (vout = 0 V) and

compute the peak voltage charged across capacitor C (VC = Vm).

- **### 3. Step 3:** Switch to the opposite half-cycle; the diode turns OFF (open circuit), and the charged

capacitor behaves like a fixed DC battery of value VC.

- **### 4. Step 4:** Apply Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL) around the outer loop to find vout = vin ± VC.
