### 2. Kirchhoff’s Laws (KCL and KVL)

#### 2.1 Fundamental Circuit Topology Terms

* **Node:** Any connection point where two or more circuit elements meet (a **Junction** is a node where 3 or more branches meet).
* **Branch:** A single path containing an element or elements connecting two adjacent nodes.
* **Loop:** Any closed conducting path in an electrical network.
* **Mesh:** An elementary loop containing NO other closed loops inside it.

#### 2.2 Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL — Point / Node Law)

* **Statement:** The algebraic sum of all electric currents meeting at any node (junction) in an electrical circuit is always zero:
$$\sum I = 0$$
* **Alternative Form:** Sum of all currents entering a node = Sum of all currents leaving that node:
$$\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$$
* **Conservation Principle Behind KCL:** Based strictly on the **Law of Conservation of Electric Charge** (charge cannot accumulate or vanish at a wire junction).

<example title="KCL at Node N" level="easy">
If currents $I_1 = 6\text{ A}$ and $I_2 = 4\text{ A}$ enter node N, while $I_3 = 3\text{ A}$ and unknown current $I_4$ leave node N:
$$6 + 4 = 3 + I_4 \implies 10 = 3 + I_4 \implies I_4 = 7\text{ A}$$
</example>

#### 2.3 Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL — Mesh / Loop Law)

* **Statement:** The algebraic sum of all voltages (EMFs and $IR$ potential drops) around any closed loop in an electrical circuit is always zero:
$$\sum V = 0$$
* **Conservation Principle Behind KVL:** Based strictly on the **Law of Conservation of Electrical Energy** (a unit charge returning to its starting node gains and loses equal net potential energy).
* **Standard Sign Convention:**
  * Moving from $-$ to $+$ across an active battery = **Voltage Rise** ($+V$).
  * Moving from $+$ to $-$ across a battery = **Voltage Drop** ($-V$).
  * Moving with assumed loop current through a resistor $R$ = **Voltage Drop** ($-IR$).
  * Moving against assumed current through a resistor $R$ = **Voltage Rise** ($+IR$).

<example title="Series KVL Loop Analysis" level="easy">
A $20\text{ V}$ DC source feeds two series resistors $R_1 = 4\,\Omega$ and $R_2 = 6\,\Omega$.
Applying KVL clockwise around the loop:
$$+20 - 4I - 6I = 0 \implies 10I = 20 \implies I = 2\text{ A}$$
Voltage drops: $V_1 = 2 \times 4 = 8\text{ V}$, $V_2 = 2 \times 6 = 12\text{ V}$.
Verification: $+20 - 8 - 12 = 0\text{ V}$ (Energy Conserved ✓).
</example>

#### 2.4 Rapid Divider Rules (Derived from KVL & KCL)

* **Voltage Divider Rule (Series $R_1, R_2$):**
$$V_1 = V_s \times \frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2} \qquad V_2 = V_s \times \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$$
* **Current Divider Rule (Parallel $R_1, R_2$):**
$$I_1 = I_{\text{total}} \times \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} \qquad I_2 = I_{\text{total}} \times \frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2}$$
*(Notice: Opposite branch resistor appears in the numerator!)*

| Feature | Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL) | Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mathematical Formula** | $\sum I = 0$ at any Node | $\sum V = 0$ around any Closed Loop |
| **Conservation Principle** | Conservation of Electric Charge | Conservation of Electrical Energy |
| **Analysis Method Enabled** | Nodal Analysis (finds node voltages) | Mesh Analysis (finds loop currents) |
