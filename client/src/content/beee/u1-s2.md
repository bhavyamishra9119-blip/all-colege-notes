### 2. Kirchhoff’s Laws (KCL and KVL)


### 2.1 Fundamental Circuit Topology Terms

- **Node:** Any connection point where two or more circuit elements meet (a Junction connects 3 or
more branches).
- **Branch:** A single circuit element or path connecting two adjacent nodes.
- **Loop:** Any closed conducting path in a circuit; a Mesh is an elementary loop containing no other
loops inside it.


### 2.2 Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL — Point / Node Law)


> 🟢 **Statement: The algebraic sum of all electric currents meeting at any node (junction) in an**

- **electrical circuit is always zero:** ∑ I = 0.
- **Alternative Form:** Sum of currents entering a node = Sum of currents leaving that node (∑
Iin = ∑ Iout).
- **Physical Law Behind KCL:** Based strictly on the Law of Conservation of Electric Charge
(electric charge cannot accumulate or vanish at a wire junction).

> 📝 **Worked Example: If I1 = 6 A and I2 = 4 A enter node N, while I3 = 3 A and unknown I4 leave node**

- **N:** 
6 + 4 = 3 + I4 → I4 = 7 A.

### 2.3 Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL — Mesh / Loop Law)


> 🟢 **Statement: The algebraic sum of all voltages (EMFs and IR drops) around any closed loop in an**

- **electrical circuit is always zero:** ∑ V = 0.
- **Physical Law Behind KVL:** Based strictly on the Law of Conservation of Energy (a unit charge
returning to its starting node gains and loses equal net potential energy).
Standard Sign Convention (Follow One Rule Consistently!):
Moving from − to + across a battery = Voltage Rise (+V); moving + to − = Voltage Drop (−V).
Moving with the assumed current through a resistor = Voltage Drop (−IR); moving against
current = Voltage Rise (+IR).

> 📝 **Worked Example: A 20 V DC source feeds two series resistors R1 = 4 Ω and R2 = 6 Ω.**

- **KVL clockwise:** +20 − 4I − 6I = 0 → 10I = 20 → I = 2 A (V1 = 8 V, V2 = 12 V).

### 3. Superposition Theorem


### 3.1 Statement, Applicability & Key Limitation


> 🟢 **Statement: In any linear, bilateral electrical network containing two or more independent**

sources, the resultant current through (or voltage across) any branch equals the algebraic sum of
the individual currents (or voltages) produced by each independent source acting alone, while
all other independent sources are deactivated.
Source Deactivation Rules (Must Memorize!):
Independent Voltage Source → Replaced by a Short Circuit (0 V) (keep its internal resistance
if given).
Independent Current Source → Replaced by an Open Circuit (0 A) (keep its internal
resistance if given).
- **Note:** Dependent sources are NEVER deactivated.
Critical Exam Limitation (Power Rule): Superposition applies ONLY to linear quantities (Current
I and Voltage V). It CANNOT be used directly to add Power because power is non-linear (P = I2R
≠ I1
2R + I2
2R)! Always find total I = I' + I'' first, then compute P = I2R.
Worked Step-by-Step Numerical (Superposition Theorem):
- **Problem:** A 20 V voltage source is connected in series with R1 = 4 Ω, feeding a center node
connected to load RL = 4 Ω (to ground) and a 3 A current source injecting upward into the same
node. Find current IL downward through RL.

### 1. Case 1 (20 V acting alone; open-circuit the 3 A source): R1 and RL are in simple series → IL'

= 20 / (4 + 4) = 2.5 A (downward).

### 2. Case 2 (3 A acting alone; short-circuit the 20 V source): R1 = 4 Ω and RL = 4 Ω are now in

- **parallel → by Current Divider:** IL'' = 3 × 4 / (4 + 4) = 1.5 A (downward).

### 3. Total Current (Superposition): Both act downward → IL = IL' + IL'' = 2.5 + 1.5 = 4.0 A

(downward).
