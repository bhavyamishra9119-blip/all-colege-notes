### 7. DC Motor: Principle, Construction, and Working


#### 7.1 Operating Principle (Motor Action)

**Energy Conversion:** A DC Motor converts direct-current (DC) electrical energy into rotating
mechanical energy.
Fundamental Principle (Lorentz Electromagnetic Force): Whenever a current-carrying
conductor is placed inside a magnetic field, it experiences a mechanical force: F = B I L
(Newtons), where B = flux density, I = conductor current, and L = active conductor length.
Direction of Force — Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule: Stretch the Thumb, Forefinger, and Middle
finger of the Left Hand mutually perpendicular:
Forefinger → Direction of Magnetic Field (North → South).
Middle Finger → Direction of Armature Current (I).
Thumb → Direction of Mechanical Motion / Force (F).

#### 7.3 Step-by-Step Working & Concept of Back EMF (Eb)

1. **Step 1 — Magnetic Field Setup:** DC excitation energizes the stator field poles (creating N and S
poles across the air gap).
2. **Step 2 — Force Pair (Torque):** DC supply feeds current through stationary carbon brushes and
the commutator into the rotor armature conductors. Conductors under the N-pole experience a
downward force while opposite conductors under the S-pole experience an upward force
(Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule) — forming a rotating couple (Torque T).
3. **Step 3 — Commutator Reversal Action:** The instant a coil crosses from the N-pole side to the
S-pole side, the split commutator reverses current direction inside that coil — ensuring conductors
under the N-pole always push the same way, producing smooth continuous rotation!

an EMF (Faraday’s Law) that opposes the applied supply voltage V (Lenz’s Law) — called
Back EMF (Eb).

#### 8.2 The Three Speed Control Methods Explained Point-by-Point

Method 1 — Field Flux Control Method (Speeds ABOVE Rated Speed)
**Circuit Setup:** Connect a small variable resistor (Field Regulator / Rheostat) in series with the
shunt field winding.
**Working:** Increasing rheostat resistance reduces shunt field current Ish → weakens magnetic flux

Method 2 — Armature Resistance Control Method (Speeds BELOW Rated Speed)
**Circuit Setup:** Connect a heavy-duty variable resistor (Controller Rheostat Rext) directly in
series with the armature winding.
**Working:** Increasing Rext increases voltage drop Ia(Ra + Rext) → lowers net voltage Eb across

whenever mechanical load changes.
Method 3 — Armature Voltage Control / Ward-Leonard System (Wide Range Both Ways)
**Circuit Setup:** Keep shunt field excitation Φ strictly constant from a fixed DC source while feeding
the armature from a smoothly variable DC voltage supply V (traditionally a Motor–Generator
Ward-Leonard set, or modern Thyristor/Chopper drive).
**Working:** Since N ∝  V, smoothly raising V from 0 to rated voltage gives stepless speed control
from zero up to base speed; reversing voltage polarity smoothly reverses rotation!
**Pros & Cons:** Ultra-smooth, wide speed range (up to 10:1), high efficiency, regenerative braking;
**Limitation:** High initial capital cost (used in steel rolling mills, mine hoists, elevators).

### 9. Three-Phase Induction Motor: Principle, Construction, and Working


#### 9.1 Principle of Operation (Rotating Magnetic Field — RMF)

Why Called an "Induction" Motor? Just like a transformer, there is NO electrical wire or brush
connection to the rotor; rotor current is induced purely by electromagnetic induction from the
stator!

**Rotating Magnetic Field (RMF):**

When a balanced 3-phase AC supply feeds three stator windings spaced 120° apart around the
stator bore, the vector sum of their three pulsating fluxes forms a single smooth magnetic field
of constant strength (1.5 Φm) rotating continuously at Synchronous Speed:

$$Ns = 120 f$$

P (RPM), where f = supply frequency (Hz) and P = number of stator poles (e.g., for 4-
**pole, 50 Hz:** Ns = 120 × 50 / 4 = 1500 RPM).
**Reversing Direction:** Simply swap any two of the three supply phase wires (e.g., swap Y and
B)!

#### 9.2 Construction of a 3-Phase Induction Motor

1. **Stator (Stationary Outer Part):** Cast-iron frame enclosing a slotted laminated silicon-steel core
carrying balanced 3-phase copper windings connected in Star (Y) or Delta (Δ).

### 2. Rotor (Rotating Inner Part — Two Types):

(a) Squirrel-Cage Rotor (~90% of Industrial Motors): Laminated steel cylinder carrying thick
uninsulated copper or aluminum bars permanently shorted at both ends by solid metal End-
Rings (looks like a hamster cage). Bars are slightly skewed to reduce magnetic hum and
prevent magnetic locking (cogging). Zero brushes, zero slip rings, ultra-rugged!
**(b) Slip-Ring (Phase-Wound) Rotor:** Carries a regular 3-phase insulated star-connected
winding whose 3 ends connect to 3 brass Slip Rings on the shaft. Stationary carbon brushes
let engineers wire external starting resistors in series to achieve very high starting torque and
limited starting current.

#### 9.3 Step-by-Step Working & Concept of Slip (s)

1. **Step 1 — RMF Sweeps Rotor:** Stator produces RMF revolving at synchronous speed Ns across
the narrow air gap.
2. **Step 2 — Induced Rotor EMF & Current:** The moving RMF cuts stationary rotor bars → induces
EMF (Faraday’s Law) → because rotor bars are closed/shorted, large rotor currents flow
immediately.
3. Step 3 — Electromagnetic Torque & Rotation (Self-Starting!): Interaction between stator RMF
and rotor current exerts mechanical torque forcing the rotor to spin in the exact same direction
as the RMF (Lenz’s Law — attempting to eliminate relative motion!).

### 4. Step 4 — Why Rotor Speed N Can NEVER Reach Ns (Asynchronous Motor):

If N = Ns, relative speed (Ns − N) becomes zero → zero flux cutting → zero induced rotor EMF
→ zero torque → motor slows down!

### 10. Single-Phase Induction Motor: Principle, Construction, and Working


#### 10.1 Why a Single-Phase Induction Motor Is NOT Self-Starting

Double-Field Revolving Theory (Exam Favorite!):
1. A single-phase AC stator winding creates a pulsating (standing) magnetic field that merely
grows and reverses along one fixed axis — it does NOT rotate!
2. Mathematically, a pulsating field Φm cos(ωt) resolves into two equal rotating fields, each of
amplitude Φm / 2, rotating in opposite directions at +Ns (Forward) and −Ns (Backward).
3. At standstill (N = 0, s = 1), Forward Torque (Tf) and Backward Torque (Tb) are equal and
opposite → Net Starting Torque = Tf − Tb = 0! The rotor simply hums and refuses to turn.
4. **Running Condition:** If given an initial kick in either direction, forward slip decreases (s < 1)
while backward slip increases (2 − s), making Tf > Tb — so net torque accelerates the rotor up
to normal speed!

#### 10.2 Construction & How We Make It Self-Starting (Split-Phase Principle)

**Construction:** Uses a standard rugged Squirrel-Cage Rotor inside a slotted laminated stator
housing TWO stator windings placed 90° electrical degrees apart:
1. **Main (Running) Winding:** Thick wire, low resistance, high inductance (current Im lags
supply voltage V strongly).
2. **Auxiliary (Starting) Winding:** Connected in parallel with the main winding; designed with
either higher resistance or a series Starting Capacitor (C) so its current Ia is phase-shifted by
30° – 90° ahead of Im.
**Working (Artificial 2-Phase RMF):** Because Im and Ia are out of phase in time AND displaced 90°
in space, they create a Rotating Magnetic Field that kicks the rotor into rotation automatically!
**Role of Centrifugal Switch:** Once the rotor reaches ~75%–80% of rated speed, a spring-loaded
centrifugal switch disconnects the auxiliary starting winding (in Split-Phase and Capacitor-Start
motors) so the motor runs efficiently on the main winding alone.
